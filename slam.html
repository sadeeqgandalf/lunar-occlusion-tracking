SLAM = Simultaneous Localization and Mapping. The rover has no GPS and no map. To know where it is, it needs a map; to draw a map, it needs to know where it is. SLAM does both at once, in a loop that runs every tenth of a second:
The SLAM loop, live updates every camera frame

1 · PREDICT wheels

2 · OBSERVE camera

3 · CORRECT compare and fix

Try this
  1. Watch the blue ellipse around the rover in the map below: it is how unsure SLAM is. It grows on the far side of the loop and snaps small when the rover recognises old rocks (a loop closure).
  2. Turn the camera off. SLAM becomes wheels-only and drifts like the red rover.
  3. Make the wheels slip 20%. Red gets lost fast; blue keeps correcting.
  4. Drop the sun to 3°. Shadows hide rock features, so there are fewer checks and a bigger ellipse.
  5. In the map, switch Map by to Wheels only: the same sensor data, drawn from a wrong position, smears the rocks.

What just happened
Words
Pose
Where the rover is and which way it faces: x, y and heading.
Landmark
A rock feature the camera can find again. Its position goes into the map.
Uncertainty ellipse (±2σ)
The area the filter is 95% sure the rover (or a landmark) is in. Bigger = less sure.
Innovation
The surprise: measured minus expected. Zero means the map and pose already agree.
Loop closure
Recognising a place seen long ago. Old landmarks are well known, so the drifted pose gets pulled back.
Drift
Error that keeps adding up when nothing checks it, like adding wheel turns.
What this page simplifies

2-D only (x, y, heading). The camera is told which landmark it sees; a real rover must recognise features (data association), which is the hard part of a loop closure. EKF-SLAM keeps every landmark in one matrix, so it slows as the map grows. Modern SLAM keeps a graph of keyframes and also bends the past path at a loop closure; here only the present pose and the map move.

Real camera SLAM, measured
VGGT-SLAM (1 camera) and ORB-SLAM3 (2 cameras) run on this lab's rendered rover drives, 5 drives per sun angle, scored against exact ground truth. In simulation, two cameras were 15 to 45 times more accurate, and both kept working at a 2° sun. Details: slam/README.md, runs in slam/runs/v5 and v6.
